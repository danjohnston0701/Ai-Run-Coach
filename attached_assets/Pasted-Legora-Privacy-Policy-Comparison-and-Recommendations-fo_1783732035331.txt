Overview
The Strava Privacy Policy is effective as of 1 January 2026 , while the AI Run Coach Privacy Policy was last updated on 24 April 2026. Both policies govern fitness and activity tracking applications that
process health and location data, but they differ significantly in scope, depth, and comprehensiveness.

Recommendations for the AI Run Coach Privacy Policy
The AI Run Coach Privacy Policy is well-structured for a smaller application and demonstrates commendable transparency — particularly around AI processing. However, the following improvements are
recommended:

1. Add &quot;Legal Obligation&quot; and &quot;Vital Interests&quot; as Legal Bases
The policy currently identifies contract performance, consent, and legitimate interests as legal bases under Article 6 GDPR but omits legal obligation and vital interests. Strava includes both legal
obligation (when required to process information by law) and vital interests (when processing is needed to protect someone&#39;s life or safety) as additional legal bases. Given that the app processes
sensitive health data in real-time during physical activity, the vital interests basis in particular is relevant and should be added.
2. Appoint and Disclose a Data Protection Officer (DPO)
The AI Run Coach policy makes no mention of a DPO. Health and fitness data is expressly classified as special category data under Article 9 and processed on the basis of explicit consent . Depending on
the scale of processing, this may trigger a mandatory DPO appointment under GDPR Article 37. Even where not strictly mandatory, disclosing a DPO contact (or a privacy-dedicated contact) as Strava
does at DPO@strava.com significantly enhances user trust and regulatory credibility. At a minimum, a dedicated privacy contact should be named separately from general support.
3. Identify the Lead Supervisory Authority
Strava identifies the Data Protection Commission of Ireland as its lead supervisory authority in the EEA . The AI Run Coach policy states only that users may lodge a complaint with their local supervisory
authority , without identifying the supervisory authority with oversight of the company itself. The policy should specify which data protection authority has primary jurisdiction (e.g., the ICO in the UK, or the
relevant EEA authority), and provide a link to that authority&#39;s website.
4. Consider Appointing an Article 27 GDPR Representative
The policy acknowledges that data may be processed outside the UK/EEA, including in the United States . If AI Run Coach is not established in the UK or EEA but processes data of UK/EEA residents, it
is required under Article 27 UK GDPR / EU GDPR to designate a local representative. The policy should either confirm that such a representative has been appointed and provide their contact details, or
confirm that an exemption applies.
5. Add a Physical/Postal Address
The policy provides only an email address (support@airuncoach.live) as contact information. Strava provides full postal addresses in multiple jurisdictions . Under GDPR, data subjects are entitled to know
the identity and contact details of the data controller. A physical or registered address should be added to improve legal compliance and user trust.
6. Provide More Detailed User Rights Instructions
The policy lists rights (access, correction, deletion, export, objection, restriction, and consent withdrawal) but provides little practical guidance on how to exercise each one . Strava, by contrast, explains
specifically how to exercise each right — for example, how to download data, how to amend account settings, and what happens after account deletion . The AI Run Coach policy would benefit from similar
specificity, including information about response timeframes (GDPR requires responses within one month).
7. Address Business Acquisitions and Corporate Transfers
The AI Run Coach policy does not address what would happen to user data in the event of a sale, merger, insolvency, or acquisition. Strava expressly addresses this, noting that data may be shared or
transferred in connection with a business combination, acquisition, securities offering, bankruptcy, reorganisation, dissolution, or other similar transaction . This is a commercially and legally important
disclosure that should be added, particularly given user sensitivity around health data.
8. Add a Non-Discrimination Clause for US Users
The policy has a section on US State Privacy Rights covering California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, and Utah , but unlike Strava, which explicitly states it will not discriminate against users for
exercising any US State Privacy Law rights , AI Run Coach makes no such commitment. This non-discrimination statement is required under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and aligned state
laws, and should be added.
9. Address Authorised Agents
Strava allows users to appoint an authorised agent to exercise privacy rights on their behalf, and requires proof of agency to prevent abuse . This is a requirement under CCPA for US users and is good
practice globally. The AI Run Coach policy should include a provision addressing how authorised agents may act on behalf of users, including the verification process.
10. Expand the Cookie and Tracking Section
The policy describes only minimal tracking for authentication, preferences, and anonymised performance analytics, and confirms no advertising cookies are used . However, it gives no detail on how users
can manage cookie preferences, what types of cookies are used, or their duration. Strava links to a separate Cookie Policy and lists specific technologies including tracking pixels, SDKs, and Google
Analytics . Consideration should be given to either expanding this section or publishing a standalone Cookie Policy, which is advisable particularly for UK/EEA compliance.
11. Strengthen the Data Breach Notification Section
The policy states only that &quot;we will notify authorities and affected users where required by law&quot;, which is a bare minimum commitment. The policy would benefit from specifying the applicable timeframes
(e.g., 72 hours to the relevant supervisory authority under GDPR/UK GDPR) and what information would be provided to affected users. This would materially enhance user confidence given the sensitivity
of the health data processed.
12. Expand the US State Privacy Rights Section
The policy covers California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, and Utah , whereas Strava&#39;s policy covers a significantly broader list of US states including California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Iowa,
Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Virginia, and others . Given the rapidly expanding US state privacy law landscape, the AI Run
Coach policy should either expand this list or adopt broader language that captures all states with similar privacy laws.
