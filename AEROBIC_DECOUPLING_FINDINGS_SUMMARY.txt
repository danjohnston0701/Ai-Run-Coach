1. ✅ DEFINITION & CALCULATION
───────────────────────────────
Formula:
  hrDrift = (secondHalfHr - firstHalfHr) / firstHalfHr × 100%
  paceDrift = (secondHalfPace - firstHalfPace) / firstHalfPace × 100%
  decoupling = hrDrift + paceDrift

What It Measures:
  → Combined percentage drift of heart rate + pace from first to second half
  → Lower = better aerobic efficiency
  → Indicates how well the runner maintained pace without HR spike

Thresholds:
  < 3.0%   → Excellent aerobic efficiency
  3.0–5.0% → Good / solid aerobic base
  5.0–8.0% → Moderate / room for improvement
  ≥ 8.0%   → High / significant decoupling (fatigue)

7. ✅ EARLY VS LATE PACE/HR COMPUTATION
────────────────────────────────────────
Split Method: CHRONOLOGICAL MIDPOINT (not distance-based)

Algorithm:
  val hrMid = hr.size / 2
  val firstHalfHr = hr.subList(0, hrMid).average()
  val secondHalfHr = hr.subList(hrMid, hr.size).average()
  
  val paceMid = paces.size / 2
  val firstHalfPace = paces.subList(0, paceMid).average()
  val secondHalfPace = paces.subList(paceMid, paces.size).average()

Why This Approach:
  ✓ Simple and deterministic
  ✓ Works for any run distance
  ✓ Captures temporal first-half vs second-half effort
  ��� No weighting bias toward fast/slow portions

Example (10 km Run, 5 km = ~25 min each):
  First Half Duration:  0–25 min (HR samples 0–75 of 150 total)
  Second Half Duration: 25–50 min (HR samples 75–150 of 150 total)

Example (5 km Run, 2.5 km each):
  First Half:  1st 2–3 km splits
  Second Half: Last 2–3 km splits

Edge Cases:
  - Negative split (faster 2nd half): Can result in negative decoupling
  - Uneven split sizes: Handled with subList range adjustment
  - Small datasets: Caught by validation (< 2 splits = no card)
